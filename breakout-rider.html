What it's actually looking for

Four shapes on a chart. Nothing else counts.

Every candidate gets sorted into one of these — or thrown out. If a stock's chart doesn't look like one of these, it doesn't matter how exciting the ticker is.

Flat base breakout

Weeks of tight, sideways chop right under a level, then it finally clears it.

Ascending triangle

Each pullback holds a higher low against the same flat ceiling — until the ceiling gives.

Breakout, then it holds

Clears the level and stays there — the follow-through that separates this from a fake-out.

Spike and fade — what it avoids

A sharp move through the level that can't hold and gives it all back. This is the shape the screen is built to reject.

How it decides

Four checks, every session, before anything gets suggested.

01

Read the market

Checks whether stocks broadly are trending up, down, or sideways first — the bar for a good setup gets stricter when the backdrop is weak.

02

Scan for bases, not spikes

Screens hundreds of stocks for ones consolidating in a tight range under a clear resistance level — then explicitly throws out anything that's already spiked through on the day without holding. Chasing an extended breakout is the most common way this kind of trade goes wrong.

03

Score it, don't just spot it

Base tightness, resistance strength, volume on the break, reward-to-risk, relative strength — scored out of 80. Below the bar, it gets dropped, even if it "looks" interesting.

04

You decide, every time

If something clears the bar, the alert publishes with the entry, stop, target, and reward:risk laid out, plus a general risk-sizing rule of thumb — never a number calculated from your account, since Zelos never sees it. What you do with that information, in your own brokerage account, is entirely your call.
