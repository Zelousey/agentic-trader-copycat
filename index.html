Why "Zelos"

It's not envy. It's knowing you're capable.

“Emulation is pain caused by seeing the presence, in persons whose nature is like our own, of good things that are highly valued and are possible for ourselves to acquire; but it is felt not because others have these goods, but because we have not got them ourselves.” — Aristotle, Rhetoric, Book II, Ch. 11 (on ζῆλος / zelos, “emulation”)

Aristotle drew a sharp line between two feelings that look similar from the outside. Envy wants the other person to lose what they have. Zelos — emulation — wants what a capable peer has because you can see, clearly, that it's within reach for you too. He called it a feeling good people have.

That's the distinction this is named after. Not chasing a stock because someone else is up on it — that's the trade every Zelos strategy explicitly refuses to make. Going after the same kind of opportunity, on your own terms, when the setup is actually there for you.

What's next

Built for more than this, if the licensing lines up.

What you see today is deliberately narrow on purpose: rule-based strategies, the same published alert for every subscriber, and no account connection of any kind. That's the foundation, not the ceiling.

🔒

Optional account-connected execution. Linking your own brokerage account so a qualifying setup can be reviewed and placed with your approval.

🔒

Sizing based on your full picture. Position sizing that accounts for your broader goals and risk tolerance, not one formula for everyone.

🔒

More than one strategy running for you. Multiple approaches allocated across your account instead of a single scan.

🔒

An actual advisory relationship. The accountability, disclosure, and fiduciary duty that come with being a licensed adviser.

None of this is available today, and it won't be until it's actually done properly — not before. Until then, every alert is general information — you decide what, if anything, to do with it.
How this works

Each Zelos strategy is its own Claude skill that scans the market on a fixed schedule, scores what it finds against a published checklist, and posts the result as a public alert — the same setup, the same entry/stop/target, shown to every subscriber at the same time. Zelos never connects to your brokerage account, never sees your balance or positions, and never places a trade.

No alert here is personalized investment advice, and Zelos is not a registered investment adviser. Trading involves risk, including loss of principal. Past performance of any strategy does not guarantee future results. "Coming soon" strategies are not available for subscription yet and no launch date is promised.
