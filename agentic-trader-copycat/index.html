Why "Zelos"

It's not envy. It's knowing you're capable.

“Emulation is pain caused by seeing the presence, in persons whose nature is like our own, of good things that are highly valued and are possible for ourselves to acquire; but it is felt not because others have these goods, but because we have not got them ourselves.” — Aristotle, Rhetoric, Book II, Ch. 11 (on ζῆλος / zelos, “emulation”)

Aristotle drew a sharp line between two feelings that look similar from the outside. Envy wants the other person to lose what they have. Zelos — emulation — wants what a capable peer has because you can see, clearly, that it's within reach for you too. He called it a feeling good people have.

That's the distinction this is named after. Not chasing a stock because someone else is up on it — that's the trade every Zelos strategy explicitly refuses to make. Going after the same kind of opportunity, on your own terms, when the setup is actually there for you. The pain isn't watching someone else win. It's sitting on the sidelines when the door was genuinely open.

How this works

Every Zelos agent is trading software you install and run yourself, as a Claude skill connected to your own brokerage account using your own credentials — we never store your account information, positions, or trade history on our own servers. Every subscriber to a given agent runs the same rule-based strategy; trade sizing is calculated automatically from your own account balance, nothing more.

No agent here is personalized investment advice, and none of them are a registered investment adviser. Trading involves risk, including loss of principal.
